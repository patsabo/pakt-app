Privacy policy

Pakt is built to keep as little as it needs, in the EU, with no ads and no tracking.

Last updated 11 October 2026.

Who is responsible

Pakt is made by Patrick Sæbø, Norway, who is the data controller for the personal data the app processes. Contact: patrick.alfei.sabo@gmail.com.

Before you have an account

Pakt opens without an account. Your drafts, settings and city stay on your phone, and nothing is sent to our server, until you sign your first contract and sign in with Apple.

What your friends see

You become friends only by giving someone your invite, or using theirs: there's no directory and no search. Your friends see the name you chose, your town hall, and the goals you set to friends or public: their outcome, area, size, deadline and how far each building has come. They never see your signature, your why, your evidence, your confidence, your plan, your notes, or a goal that failed. Each goal's visibility can be changed at any time. Anyone can remove, block or report a friend; a report is read by the person who runs Pakt, who can hide a goal from friends or close an account that breaks the community rules.

When you do a goal together, everyone in it sees each other's names, how far each building has come and who has finished, and in a joint goal, who checked off each shared step. That includes people in it who aren't your friends. Your why, plan, confidence and signature stay yours.

Why we're allowed to

  • Your account, goals, settings, devices and purchases: to provide the app you asked for (GDPR article 6(1)(b)).
  • The coach: your explicit consent (articles 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a)), because a goal can say something about your health, beliefs or relationships. You can withdraw it at any time in the town hall.
  • Notifications: your permission on the phone, and a switch per type in the town hall.
  • Consent records: to show what you agreed to (article 7(1)).
  • Friends: to provide the feature you chose (article 6(1)(b)). Reports and blocks: our legitimate interest in keeping friends safe (article 6(1)(f)).
  • Technical logs: our legitimate interest in keeping the service secure (article 6(1)(f)).

How long

We keep your data until you delete your account. Deleting it is immediate and complete: Pakt revokes its access at Apple and removes everything above from our server; your other phones wipe themselves the next time they connect. Withdrawn consents stop the processing at once.

Two small records outlast a deletion, and neither holds anything you wrote:

  • For 8 days, the ID of a deleted account or goal and when it was deleted, so that if we ever have to restore our database from a backup, we can delete it again before anyone uses the restored data.
  • For 3 years, any action an administrator took on your account (for example running an export you asked for by email), with the reason. This record shows that support access was legitimate, and 3 years is Norway's general limitation period, the time in which a complaint or claim about how data was handled can arise. After your account is deleted, it holds only an ID that no longer leads to you.

Some friends records go sooner: an invite a month after it was used or ran out, and a code that was looked up but not found after a day. A report is kept until it's been dealt with and a year after that; if the person who made it deletes their account, the report stays without them.

Today our database has no backups, so a deletion is final at once. When we start taking backups, they will be kept for 7 days, and deleted data in them is never used again.

Your rights

You can see, export and delete your data yourself in the app (the town hall). You also have the right to correction, restriction, objection and data portability, and to withdraw any consent. Write to patrick.alfei.sabo@gmail.com for anything the app doesn't do for you; we answer within a month. If you think we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to Datatilsynet, the Norwegian Data Protection Authority (datatilsynet.no).

Children

Pakt is for people 13 and over. When the year of birth shows someone is younger, the app stops and keeps nothing. People under 18 can have friends, who are found only through an invite, and share goals with them, but their goals can't be made public.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the app tells you and asks again where your consent is needed.
